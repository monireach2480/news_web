Vercel Breach Tied to Context AI Hack Exposes Limited Customer Credentials

April 20, 2026 Tech News Team 6 min read
Breaches Threats

Vercel reported a security breach caused by the compromise of Context.ai, a third-party AI tool used by an employee. Attackers gained access through a compromised OAuth token, allowing them to take over the employee's Google Workspace account and access parts of Vercel's systems, including some non-sensitive environment variables. Sensitive data remained protected due to encryption, and only a limited number of customers were affected, though some credentials were exposed. The attacker is considered highly skilled, and investigations are ongoing with cybersecurity firms. The breach may have started from malware (Lumma Stealer) infecting a Context.ai employee. Vercel and Context.ai have both implemented security improvements and advised users to enable multi-factor authentication, review activity, and rotate credentials. This incident highlights growing risks from OAuth tokens and third-party integrations in modern supply chains. Read More

CISA Adds One Known Exploited Vulnerability to Catalog

April 18, 2026 Tech News Team 5 min read
Vulnerabilities Compliance

CISA has added one new vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) Catalog, based on evidence of active exploitation. CVE-2026-35616 - Fortinet FortiClient EMS Improper Access Control Vulnerability This type of vulnerability is a frequent attack vector for malicious cyber actors and poses significant risks to the federal enterprise. Binding Operational Directive (BOD) 22-01: Reducing the Significant Risk of Known Exploited Vulnerabilities established the KEV Catalog as a living list of known Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVEs) that carry significant risk to the federal enterprise. BOD 22-01 requires Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB) agencies to remediate identified vulnerabilities by the due date to protect FCEB networks against active threats. See the BOD 22-01 Fact Sheet for more information. Although BOD 22-01 only applies to FCEB agencies, CISA strongly urges all organizations to reduce their exposure to cyberattacks by prioritizing timely remediation of KEV Catalog vulnerabilities as part of their vulnerability management practice. CISA will continue to add vulnerabilities to the catalog that meet the specified criteria. Read More

TfL hack in 2024 affected around 10 million people, BBC can reveal

April 15, 2026 Tech News Team 5 min read
Breaches Threats

A massive cyberattack on Transport for London (TfL) in 2024 exposed the personal data of about 10 million people, making it one of the biggest hacks in UK history. Initially, TfL said only "some" users were affected, but it later confirmed the full scale. The attack was carried out by the Scattered Spider hacking group, who broke into TfL's internal systems and stole a customer database. This caused around £39 million in damages and disrupted online services, though public transport itself wasn't directly affected. Two British teenagers accused of the hack are scheduled to go on trial in June. Read More

Misuse of information and communications technologies

April 12, 2026 Tech News Team 6 min read
Threats Best Practices

There is growing concern over the misuse of information and communications technologies (ICT) by terrorists, in particular the Internet and new digital technologies, to commit, incite, recruit, fund or plan terrorist acts. Member States have stressed the importance of multi-stakeholder cooperation in tackling this threat, including among international, regional and subregional organizations, the private sector and civil society in Security Council resolution 2341 (2017) and the United Nations Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy (GCTS). In the seventh review of the GCTS, the Office of Counter-Terrorism and other relevant Global Counter-Terrorism Coordination Compact entities were requested to "jointly support innovative measures and approaches to build the capacity of Member States, upon their request, for the challenges and opportunities that new technologies provide, including the human rights aspects, in preventing and countering terrorism."

The concern started after Anthropic introduced a new AI feature for its Claude model that can scan code for vulnerabilities and suggest fixes. Major cybersecurity firms like CrowdStrike and Zscaler dropped around 10%, while others like Netskope, Tenable, Okta, and Palo Alto Networks also saw significant losses. Cybersecurity ETFs dropped as well.
